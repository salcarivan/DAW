## 1 Build a Ready-to-Use Worksheet
> "Create a purposeful classroom worksheet for [subject] for [grade/age/level] on [topic], aligned with these learning objectives: [insert objectives], prior learning: [insert details], and available time: [duration]. Use clear, student-friendly instructions and sequence tasks logically from supported to independent practice. Include a worked example or model, then gradually remove the scaffolding. Vary the tasks so students build understanding and apply it, not just repeat the same thing. Match the difficulty and workload to the group. Finish with a task that checks whether the objectives were actually met. Present it in a clean, classroom-ready format using tables, boxes, diagrams, or answer spaces where useful, and include an answer key."
Why it works: Forces Claude to build in
progression (supported → independent) instead of dumping 20 identical questions, which is the biggest flaw in generic worksheets.

## 4 Build Reading & Comprehension Packs
> "Create reading and comprehension materials for [subject] for [grade/age/level] on [topic], aligned with these learning objectives: [insert objectives] and students" reading level: [insert level]. Provide or adapt a text at the right challenge level, then build activities using strategies like predicting, questioning, clarifying, inferring, finding the main idea, summarizing, or mapping information visually, picking only what fits the text. Include questions that require evidence from the text, not just guesses. Present it clearly with good text layout, answer spaces, tables, or graphic organizers, and include an answer key."
Why it works: Pulls from real reading
comprehension research (reciprocal teaching strategies) instead of generic "read and answer" tasks.

## 5 Spark Deeper Thinking with Discussion Questions
> "Create purposeful questions for [subject] for [grade/age/level] on [topic], aligned with these learning objectives: [insert objectives] and prior learning: [insert details]. Sequence questions from checking basic understanding to deeper thinking, asking students to explain, justify, compare, apply, or evaluate. Require reasons, evidence, or examples, not one-word answers. Add discussion prompts that push students to consider other viewpoints and respond to classmates. Match the language and challenge level to the group. Present clearly, with suggested answers or key discussion points included."
Why it works: Stops discussions from dying after one question by building in follow-up prompts that force real back-and-forth.

Present the final resource as a clean, classroom-ready visual students can use to learn, organize, or review."

# 7 Build Smart Revision Packs
> "Create revision and review materials for [subject] for [grade/age/level] covering [unit/topics], aligned with these learning objectives: [insert objectives] and areas requiring further review: [insert details]. Include a mix of retrieval questions, w, flashcards, short explanation questions, and mixed practice. Cover important learning from across the whole unit, not just the last lesson taught. Give students chances to explain, apply, and connect ideas. Prioritize weak areas while still covering all key content. Present it clearly for independent or guided revision, with an answer key included."
Why it works: Bakes in "cumulative review," the single most underused revision technique, instead of last-minute cramming on recent topics only.
